Pattern Mode: patterns change the rules

Version 1 designed loop selection for the single-grid world, with Capture as a bridge toward patterns. That got it backwards. Once patterns and chaining exist, they are the ground truth: looping stops being a stored region and becomes what's cycling right now; pages become vestigial because patterns are the pages; the mini-map inverts from a spatial overview into the chain timeline; and viewing splits from playback — you edit B while everyone hears A. The v1 lessons (honest geometry, one membership truth, tap-first grammar, visuals never contradict audio) all survive; the surfaces they produce are different.

Rule change 1

Loop is a mode, not a region. A groovebox is always looping something — the playing pattern by default, a braced slice of the chain while arranging, a held bar while performing. Stored step-ranges disappear.

Rule change 2

Patterns replace pages. Structure stops living at step 17–48 of a mega-grid and starts living in short patterns seen whole. Long-grid coping tools — page chips, region dimming, overview scrolling — dissolve rather than get fixed.

Rule change 3

View ≠ playback. The grid shows the viewed pattern; sound follows the playing one. Playheads render only where sound is — progress elsewhere lives on the playing chip, never as a ghost playhead on a silent grid.

Rule change 4

The chain is the timeline. Arrangement is chips-with-repeats, not a clip timeline. The loop brace moves up a level: it wraps chain entries, which is what "loop pages 2–3" was always trying to be.
