Pattern & Song

v3 was conceptually overloaded — split-zone chips, view≠play, chain braces, scenes. Forty-five years of drum machines and trackers converged on something far simpler: two concepts (a pattern you compose, a song that is an ordered list of patterns) and one question (does Play play the pattern or the song?). The TR-808's Compose mode, the LinnDrum and MPC's Song, and the tracker order list 00 01 00 02 are all this model. The famous confusions — the TB-303's hidden modes, Elektron's unsaved chains, Circuit's scene ladder — are all departures from it. v4 adopts the classic model outright: the grid always shows what's sounding, slots switch patterns (cued at the loop boundary, like a 1980 pattern button), and the song is a list you edit like a list.

Concept 1 · Pattern

A short loop you compose. Slots 1–8, whole-grid snapshots. Exactly one is current — synced, shown, heard, edited by everyone. Tap a slot to switch; while playing it cues and lands at the loop end, blinking until it does.

Concept 2 · Song

An ordered list of patterns. Rows of pattern × repeats, exactly a tracker's order list. Add, reorder, repeat, delete. No chains, no braces, no scenes — the list is the arrangement.

One question

What does Play play? PATTERN loops the current slot; SONG plays the list top to bottom, deterministically. The 808's mode dial, reborn as a two-position toggle beside Play.

One invariant

What you see is what you hear. The grid always shows the sounding pattern — in SONG scope it follows the list. No view/play split, no ghost playheads, no second cursor. Multiplayer keeps its existing rule: everyone sees and hears the same thing.

1One slot, one action. Slots are plain buttons (keys 1–8): tap to switch patterns — instantly when stopped, cued to the loop end while playing. Current = orange with the loop's progress bar. No split zones, no view-vs-queue halves.
2The song is a vertical order list — the tracker's 00 01 00 02, humanized: each row is a pattern (with its real thumbnail) × a repeat count. Drag to reorder, tap ×N to change repeats. The playing row is highlighted. This replaces v3's chain strip and loop braces entirely.
3The grid follows the sound. In SONG scope the grid always shows the pattern that's sounding (P2, playhead live) — tracker "follow" behavior. Editing during song playback edits what you hear, exactly like today's app.
4Two verbs, both historical: ⧉ Duplicate = every drum machine's pattern copy (make a variation, then switch). ✂ Split = the migration tool — explodes a legacy 64/128-step pattern into slots + a song that plays back identically.
5Want to woodshed one section? Flip the scope to PATTERN on that slot — that's "loop a section while composing," no braces required.
