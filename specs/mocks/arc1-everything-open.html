What's open

1A dropdown (Invite ▾) — the one portal component; only one can exist at a time by construction.
2One aux panel (FX). MIX and PITCH are the same chip row — opening one closes the others. Today all three can be open simultaneously, stacked.
3One inspector, inline under Bass, with its steps ▾ dropdown also open. Escape closes the dropdown, then the inspector — the cascade.
4Every track's property lane — Kick and Bass on VEL, Keys on PITCH. These are concurrent: one lane per track, its property chosen by the chip.
5Live state on top: a 3-step selection (blue), a remote attribution flash (green), a mute (yellow), a playhead (white), and a toast naming the track by its tick.

What can't be open

✕A second aux panel — chip rows are one-of-N.
✕A second inspector — disclose is single-target, so the Escape cascade always has one unambiguous next step.
✕Pattern tools / pitch view / velocity as three separate stacked panels per track — that was today's arrangement; now the lane is one row and tools live in the inspector.

The density ledger

worst case, Arc 1 vs today

Counting simultaneously open surfaces for a three-track session with everything expanded. Today each track can stack a pitch view, a velocity lane and a pattern-tools panel independently, while the transport can hold FX, Mixer and Pitch at once.

Today · worst case13

3 transport panels + (3 tracks × 3 panels) + 1 drawer. Roughly 3× the viewport height, and the grid is pushed off-screen.

Arc 1 · worst case5

1 aux panel + 3 property lanes + 1 inspector. Every track's cells stay visible — the frame above is the worst case.

Bounded by design2

Only two things scale with track count (lanes, and nothing else). Panels are one-of-N; disclose is single-target.

Surfaces per track3 → 1

Pitch view, velocity lane and pattern tools become one lane (property-switched) plus the shared inspector.

The claim this tests: consolidation isn't just fewer control types — it caps how bad the screen can get. In Arc 1 the maximum is a constant (1 panel + 1 inspector) plus one row per track, so the grid never leaves the screen no matter how much is open.

One decision this mock forces

needs your call before Arc 1
Is the chromatic grid a property lane, or its own surface? Pitch is a per-step property (parameterLocks[i].pitch), so the tidy answer is the one drawn above: the lane's property chip switches between VEL (bars) and PITCH (a grid of note rows), which makes ♪ and ▎ collapse into a single toggle and keeps every per-step editor in one place, column-aligned. The counter-argument: the chromatic grid is a note-entry surface with its own vertical axis, scale colouring, and Grid/PianoRoll modes — it is much taller than a lane and arguably a different job. If you prefer it separate, the row keeps two toggles (LANE and ♪), the worst case rises from 5 surfaces to 8, and C-4's "13 → 6 controls" becomes "13 → 7". Drawn here as the consolidated option because it's the one that has to be argued out of, not into, the plan.
Read the frame as a promise: this is as busy as Arc 1 ever gets.
